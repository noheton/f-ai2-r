# Cover letter — Quantitative Science Studies

Dear Editors,

I submit "F(AI)²R: Who Did What, and Who Checked? Verifiable AI
Provenance as an Executable Skill" for consideration as a research
article in Quantitative Science Studies.

AI systems now draft manuscripts, sweep literature, and verify
results, yet their contributions are rarely recorded in a form a later
human or machine can audit. The manuscript treats this as a
scholarly-communication problem: as generation becomes free, the
scarce good is a checkable record of who did what and who vouched for
it. It contributes (i) aiprov, a PROV-O extension whose verification
ladder reserves its top rungs for humans; (ii) the method packaged as
an executable skill that an AI agent operates itself; and (iii) a
fully measured self-demonstration: the paper's own production is
recorded in a public provenance graph, and every reported number is
machine-derived from that record, with the underlying usage telemetry
published for independent recomputation.

The fit with the journal's focus on the system of science is direct:
the demonstration quantifies method overhead, computed cost with a
disclosed price basis, and the human–AI division of work, and it
engages the transparency expectations of the EU AI Act. Limitations
(a single operator and domain; self-confirmation bias) are stated and
measured rather than waived away: the paper presents an idea in use,
not a completed study.

A preprint is available on arXiv; the repository and a checksummed
release are public (github.com/noheton/f-ai2-r, release v0.17.0),
and the data essential for reproduction, graph, metrics, telemetry,
and transcripts, are archived on Zenodo as cited in the data
availability statement. The manuscript is not under consideration
elsewhere. It was produced with an AI agent
operating the described method; the disclosure statement it carries
is generated from the record itself.

Thank you for your consideration.
