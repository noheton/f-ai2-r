\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Self-report honesty.} The graph is only as truthful as its
writer; Section~\ref{sec:method}'s commit binding, server-side
validation, omit-don't-estimate rule and human-only ceiling are
mitigations already stated, not add-ons. The overwrite incident of
Section~\ref{sec:meta} shows them working: an AI mistake was a
recoverable, recorded event rather than silent corruption.

\textbf{From honesty to evidence: the dual-witness ask.} The record
has one witness: telemetry is provider-generated but agent-relayed,
so nothing stops a dishonest agent from underreporting usage,
omitting sessions, or inventing activities. Provider-side signed
inference records, carrying model, timestamps, usage, roles, and
prompt/response hashes, would eliminate that class of misreporting
and subsume this paper's other provider asks (cost, energy,
unambiguous roles), but not be tamper-\emph{proof}: trust shifts
rather than vanishes (key custody moves to the provider); coverage
stops at the API seam (a dishonest client can still mis-bind a
genuine inference to the wrong artefact); and judgements stay
judgements (a signature proves a model emitted a verdict, not that
it is sound, and none can attest that a human read a source). The
realistic endpoint is a \emph{dual witness}: provider-signed
inference logs and repository-side commit binding, each referencing
the other's hashes, so falsifying the record requires collusion
between independent, separately published histories, the trust
structure of certificate transparency. A third witness is nearly
free: anchoring release checksums in an append-only public log makes
retroactive rewriting detectable without trusting the host, though
anchoring, like signing, certifies existence, never truth. aiprov
supplies one witness and the seam for the others; signed provenance
is the strongest standing ask this paper directs at providers.

\textbf{Why human-only rungs stay human.} \texttt{ai-confirmed} is a
machine judgement with known failure
modes~\cite{ai-hallucination2023}. The ladder does not pretend
otherwise; it makes the judgement's author explicit instead of
laundering machine confidence into apparent human endorsement. The
human rung is no formality either: even domain experts struggle to
override erroneous AI judgements against contradicting
evidence~\cite{doctors-vs-algorithms2026}; hence the rung-4 gate
hands the operator source and citing sentence, evidence before
judgement. There is also a limit here, and it is worth stating
plainly: what \texttt{human-read} records is \emph{that} a named
person vouched, on a date, over identified evidence, not that they
read carefully, understood correctly, or judged well, which is not
itself machine-checkable and no additional rung could make it so. The
human-only rungs are therefore not a claim that people are more
reliable than machines; they mark the boundary where structural
verification ends and attributable judgement begins, and a recorded
refusal to promote is the method's form of silence at that boundary.

\textbf{Cost, and a gap that half closed.} The method's overhead in
this experiment was one logging command and one extra commit per
working round, plus the audit activities the tooling writes itself.
Because the session record carries per-request usage and tool calls,
that overhead is measurable rather than anecdotal: a mechanical
classification of every provider request by its tool invocations,
bookkeeping commands (logging, validation, promotion, graph and
transcript commits) versus everything else, attributes
\MOverReqPct{} of requests, \MOverOutPct{} of output tokens, and
\MOverCostPct{} of the computed session cost to the method itself
(Table~\ref{tab:division}); on the repository side, \MBookCommits{}
of \MCommits{} commits carried only graph or transcript updates. The
classification is a floor, not a ceiling: it counts a request as
bookkeeping only when every one of its tool calls is, so a request
that mixes a logging command with substantive work counts entirely
toward the other side, and the true overhead share is at least this
large. The graph preserves the distinction
between \emph{reported} telemetry (token counts from the provider's
usage blocks) and \emph{computed} telemetry (cost, derived from them
at a disclosed price list); computed values are honest only while
their basis is recorded, provider-reported cost would still be the
stronger record. A larger question stays open behind the numbers:
this session consumed \MCacheReadMint~million cache-read tokens,
USD~\MCostUSD{} in all at list prices, for one draft paper and one
method release, and whether that justifies its environmental and
other costs is a debate this paper does not settle and does not hide,
only insists belongs over recorded consumption, not guesses in either
direction. The graph makes the bill legible; whether it is worth
paying is a judgement the record enables but cannot make.

\textbf{A practice that emerged: content-checked before load-bearing.}
Registry-fetched metadata, ORCID-resolved identity, and a
ladder-backed source for every citation are the method's own
invariants (Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:skill}), not
discoveries. One discipline is: born from this paper's own audit
(Section~\ref{sec:meta}), any citation an argument leans on should
reach \texttt{ai-confirmed} at minimum, with the checked depth
disclosed in the promotion note, not registration alone.

\textbf{Transparent self-citation.} No-parentless-claim forces citing
one's own priors, the lineage of Section~\ref{sec:background}, and
self-citation is frowned upon as metric gaming yet unavoidable as
provenance. The resolution is transparency instead of omission:
sources carry \texttt{aiprov:selfCitation}, the report prints the
ratio (\MSrcSelf{} of \MSources), \MSrcSelfHuman{} already
operator-confirmed, the rest flagged link-only until then. The test
that separates influence from inflation: a transparent self-citation
is load-bearing, removing it would orphan a lineage or method claim,
and it points at repositories and specifications, not metric-bearing
venues. It declares descent; it does not farm citations.

\textbf{Regulatory alignment.} The EU AI Act asks for disclosure and
machine-readable marking of AI-generated content~\cite{eu-ai-act2024};
a provenance graph is exactly that marking, and because CI derives
the disclosure statement from the graph on every build, the published
statement cannot lag the record. The labelling duty for published
text falls away under human review with editorial responsibility,
which is what the ladder records~\cite{eu-ai-icons2026}, so the graph
substantiates label and exemption alike. Research-integrity guidance
asks the same from the norms side: tool, version, date, and
manner~\cite{faq-ai-gwp2025}; a graph-derived disclosure meets that
core mechanically, except date and manner, not yet generated
(Table~\ref{tab:align}). Alignment reaches beyond the EU
(Table~\ref{tab:align}); provenance-first authoring turns a
compliance duty into a by-product.

\textbf{Generality, bounded.} The same invariants and ladder apply
beyond papers, to code, data pipelines, CAD models, and AAS
submodels~\cite{aas-part1-v30rc02}; only the entity subclasses are
domain work. The skill bundle follows the open Agent Skills
format~\cite{agent-skills-spec}, so it loads in any client that
implements it, and the graph is provider-agnostic, since any AI
system, hosted or local, registers as an \texttt{AIAgent} with model
and provider as data, though local-model identity (weights hash,
quantization, runtime) is still planned. The limits are concrete:
three capture shims are client-specific (turn-end hook, transcript
export, usage-field mapping, the last written against one client's
format), and today's graph records one AI-agent client. Graph,
ladder, and validator stay plain Python and git regardless; AI-work
provenance is one more submodel concern, not a foreign add-on.

\textbf{Industry relevance: when the AI's output is a decision.} The
stakes sharpen outside publishing: in manufacturing, AI increasingly
prepares or makes operational decisions, releasing a weld,
classifying a part, adjusting a process window, one step from the
worked example of Section~\ref{sec:skill}. The graph answers what
certification and liability ask of such high-risk systems: which
agent decided, on which evidence, under which model version, and who
signed off (Table~\ref{tab:align}). The human-only rungs map directly
onto release decisions that must stay with people.

\textbf{Originality of this work, on both sides of the division.}
Section~\ref{sec:dilution} demands that novelty claims be bounded and
checkable; the demand applies to this paper first. So the claim is
made by the method's own mechanism: a logged novelty sweep refreshed
the search horizon, its nearest find is named and cited, and the
recorded claim is deliberately narrow. Per-activity AI provenance on
a PROV substrate is \emph{not} new, PROV-AGENT does
that~\cite{prov-agent2025}; what no found work combines is human-only
verification rungs, executable-skill packaging, and self-application,
and that combination, within the recorded horizon, is this paper's
originality claim, refutable by widening the sweep; the original sweep
missed four neighbouring lines, and the review widened the recorded horizon.
The effort behind it divides tellingly (Section~\ref{sec:meta}'s
Division of labour): the fast side executed and recombined, which is
what models are shown to supply~\cite{llm-homogenization2024}; the
slow side, years of experiments and specification co-authoring
compressed into direction, is where the originality entered. Neither
side alone would have produced the work, and the record keeps both
visible, which is what an originality dispute would need.

\textbf{Copyright when the machine wrote most of it.} The paper
repository is Apache-2.0-licensed, the aiprov package MIT, but a
license can only grant what copyright first protects, and most of
each repository's code and prose was AI-generated under human
direction. The U.S.\ Copyright Office holds that copyright ``does not
extend to purely AI-generated material, or material where there is
insufficient human control over the expressive elements'', that
prompts alone do not provide that control, and that protection
attaches to human-authored expression and to ``the creative
selection, coordination, or arrangement'' of
outputs~\cite{usco-ai-copyrightability2025}. German law agrees from
its definition of the work: \S~2(2) UrhG protects only
\emph{pers\"onliche geistige Sch\"opfungen}~\cite{urhg-para2}, so
current LLM output as such is not copyrightable in Germany. Read
against this paper's own record: line by line, much of the artefact
may be uncopyrightable, and the license's effective scope narrows to
the human contributions and the curated whole. What the method adds
is the evidence a legal answer needs: which expression a human
conceived, selected, arranged, or modified, and which the machine
produced under how much control (Table~\ref{tab:division}), what a
blanket ``AI-assisted'' disclaimer erases. Rights analysis of AI-era
works founders on evidence before doctrine; provenance-first
authoring is how the evidence survives.

\textbf{Nostalgia for the paper, or the wrong artefact instrumented?}
The sharpest objection is about the target, not any rung: perhaps
F(AI)\textsuperscript{2}R patches provenance onto a form we are
nostalgic for, while AI-driven science becomes something structurally
different, living claim graphs, continuously re-executed analyses,
narrative generated on demand, making PDF production the wrong
artefact to instrument. But the invariants and ladder attach to
claims, sources, activities, and agents, never to a manuscript; the
PDF is still what CI builds and ships, but the claim-level form
underneath it, \MClaims{} claims with their own generating activities
and sources, already exists and could stand alone. Read that way,
F(AI)\textsuperscript{2}R is a migration path, emitting the legacy
format today while building the substance a post-paper science would
need; judge it by whether its graphs stay useful without the PDFs.

\textbf{The force multiplier, both ways, and the optimistic reading.}
One operator ran method, paper, and integration fork in parallel
during this experiment; unaccounted, the same multiplication is the
dilution engine of Section~\ref{sec:dilution}. The differentiator is
not speed but auditability: fast work that carries its record is
distinguishable from fast noise. Fast paper, slow ideas is the honest
reading of this project, and only the graph makes that reading
checkable. There is also a hopeful version of the same observation.
What the multiplication buys is not mainly volume: AI absorbing the
tedium (registry lookups, hash binding, conformance checks, build
plumbing) can free human attention for the two things machines do not
supply, connecting ideas and connecting people. This experiment's own
activity log shows that division of labour (Section~\ref{sec:meta}).
No guarantee, only a chance; a provenance graph is how one would
check, later, whether the chance was taken.

\textbf{Limitations, in one place.} One operator, one domain, one
paper; portability waits for a second. The record has a single
witness: all \MClaims{} claims sit at \texttt{ai-confirmed}, and
\texttt{ai-confirmed} judgements here were granted by the same agent
that wrote the citing sentences, a self-confirmation bias the dual-witness
ask of this section exists to address. Human rungs are granted
against the evidence handed over, \MLinkOnly{} of them still
link-only and so mutable; \MGrantsSession{} of \MHumanRungs{} grants
are checkable only against the committed transcript, not an
independent log. Telemetry covers \MActsTelemetry{} of \MActivities{}
activities (tokens on \MActsTokens, cost on \MActsCost); the energy
field is empty, \MEnergyTriples{} triples, because none was reported.
The graph records \MSessionIds{} sessions across one operator; a
single AI-agent client. None of this is hidden; all of it is why the
claims above are stated at the ceiling an AI may grant, and no
higher.
